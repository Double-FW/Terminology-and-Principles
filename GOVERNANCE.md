# Governing meanings and changes

## Purpose

Keep meanings consistent while preserving justified differences, human language preferences and the authority of original sources.

## Ownership and review

Double FW Ltd owns the original glossary material. Gareth, or a person explicitly delegated by him, is the glossary approval owner. A subject-matter reviewer should examine consequential specialist changes. Gareth has now supplied the decisions recorded in review/OWNER_DECISIONS_2026-11-20.md. No independent specialist review or delegation is inferred.

## Authority and conflict handling

1. Identify the actual task, context and source of authority. A standard term, a legal term, a client policy and a Double-FW design lens can have different roles.
2. Preserve the precise external meaning when quoting or claiming conformance with an external requirement. Do not substitute a preferred Double-FW definition into a statute or standard.
3. For Double-FW methodologies, use the explicitly adopted Double-FW interpretation and name any departure from external approaches. A draft glossary does not silently supersede the owning methodology.
4. Preserve approved local organisational decisions in their proper scope. Neither a repository publication nor a glossary edit authenticates a client's approval.
5. Where meanings disagree, record both, the affected sources, the proposed resolution, consequences and the human decision required. Proceed with unaffected work and mark the affected conclusion unresolved.

## Review states

**Proposed for owner review → approved → deprecated**, with **disputed** available where agreement is unresolved. Approval means acceptance of that glossary wording for a stated scope. It does not establish scientific validation, statutory correctness, framework adoption or customer outcomes.

Every approval needs a real decision record identifying the owner, date, scope, evidence, alternatives and affected records. Set approved_by and approval_date only from that record. Agents must not invent approvals. A proposal can be revised without presenting it as accepted. Deprecated entries retain their stable ID and replacement relationship.

## Versioning and history

Keep stable DFW-T identifiers. Do not reuse a retired identifier for another meaning. Source identifiers such as CP-01, IDSO-01 and ACT test references keep their namespaces and owning documents. A spelling fix can be editorial; a changed boundary or applicability can be substantive even if only one word changes.

Record material changes in the changelog and decision register. Keep previous releases and migration notes. Existing documents should be updated in checked batches, with their own examples, quoted wording and historical context preserved. Do not run indiscriminate search-and-replace across repositories.

## Language and identity

Use plain British English in explanations. Preserve proper names, official titles, technical tokens and the language a participant uses for their identity. Do not turn a preferred internal term into a rule that erases Deaf identity, neurodivergent identity or a source's statistical definition. Describe barriers and support without inferring diagnoses from behaviour.
