## Cognitive accessibility

**Reference:** DFW-T0042 · **Status:** Proposed for owner review · **Basis:** source grounded synthesis

Addressing barriers involving attention, memory, language, comprehension, learning and related demands in actual contexts.

**Scope:** Working Double-FW cross-repository terminology; preserve the owning source context.

**Distinctions and limits:** Do not infer a fixed capability profile from diagnosis, sight history or familiarity.

**Example:** Keep necessary information available instead of requiring cross-screen recall.

## Recognition rather than recall

**Reference:** DFW-T0044 · **Status:** Proposed for owner review · **Basis:** source grounded synthesis

Keeping relevant objects, actions, choices and information identifiable and retrievable instead of requiring memory.

**Scope:** Working Double-FW cross-repository terminology; preserve the owning source context.

**Distinctions and limits:** Recognition is not proof of comprehension; familiar wording can still be misunderstood.

**Example:** Show the selected account and amount before a transfer is confirmed.

**Source context:**

## Assistive technology (AT)

**Reference:** DFW-T0046 · **Status:** Proposed for owner review · **Basis:** source grounded synthesis

Technology used to support a person's access, interaction or participation in relation to disability or functional needs.

**Scope:** Working Double-FW cross-repository terminology; preserve the owning source context.

**Distinctions and limits:** Do not treat screen readers as the whole category or claim compatibility with every combination.

**Example:** Record the tested screen reader, browser, operating system and relevant settings.

## Progressive enhancement

**Reference:** DFW-T0056 · **Status:** Proposed for owner review · **Basis:** source grounded synthesis
